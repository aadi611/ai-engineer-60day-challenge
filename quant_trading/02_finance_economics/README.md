# Module 2: Finance & Economics Fundamentals

The math and stats modules gave you the tools. This module gives you the
vocabulary and mental models for what you're actually applying them to —
companies, markets, money, and the macro forces that move all of it. Without
this, you can build a statistically valid model of something you don't
actually understand, which is worse than not building it at all.

---

## 2.1 Financial accounting basics (1 week)

You need to be able to read a company's financials well enough to understand
what fundamental data means when you use it as a factor later (Module 5).

- [Khan Academy — Accounting and financial statements](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/core-finance/accounting-and-financial-statemen) (free)
- Focus: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement, and the handful of ratios that show up everywhere (P/E, P/B, ROE, debt/equity, free cash flow)

**Exercise:** pull a real company's 10-K or a summary (e.g. via a free
financial data site), and write a half-page plain-English summary of its
financial health using the three statements and key ratios.

## 2.2 Microeconomics (1 week)

- [Khan Academy — Microeconomics](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics) (free)
- Focus: supply/demand, market equilibrium, elasticity, competitive vs. monopolistic markets — this underlies *why* prices move and what "efficient market" even claims

## 2.3 Macroeconomics (1-1.5 weeks)

- [Khan Academy — Macroeconomics](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/macroeconomics) (free)
- Focus: interest rates, inflation, GDP, central bank policy (Fed funds rate, QE/QT), yield curves, business cycles

**Why this matters concretely:** interest rates alone drive bond pricing,
discount rates in valuation, FX movements, and risk-on/risk-off regime shifts
that determine which quant strategies work in which macro environment.

**Exercise:** track the yield curve (e.g. via FRED — Federal Reserve Economic
Data, free) for the last 2 years, identify any inversions, and write a short
note on what a yield curve inversion is typically interpreted to signal and why.

## 2.4 Corporate finance & valuation (1 week)

- [Khan Academy — Corporate finance / valuation](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/core-finance) (free)
- Focus: time value of money, discounted cash flow (DCF), cost of capital (WACC), the idea of "present value" as it underlies literally every pricing model you'll meet later (bonds, options, DCF valuation)

## 2.5 Market structure & the efficient market hypothesis (0.5 week)

- [Investopedia — Efficient Market Hypothesis](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/e/efficientmarkethypothesis.asp)
- Read about the three forms (weak/semi-strong/strong) and the major critiques (behavioral finance, momentum/value anomalies)

**Why this matters for you specifically:** EMH is the null hypothesis every
quant strategy is implicitly arguing against. Understanding it precisely (and
its well-documented cracks — momentum, value, size effects) tells you where
real, persistent edges have historically come from, versus where you're
likely just finding noise (ties directly back to Module 1.3's multiple
comparisons problem).

- State the efficient market hypothesis and name 2-3 documented anomalies that challenge it
